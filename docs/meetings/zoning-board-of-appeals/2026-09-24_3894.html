September 24, 2026 — Zoning Board of Appeals

CivicClerk page
Agenda PDF

Summary AI

The Zoning Board approved setback and coverage variances for a home addition in Aquebogue, and adjourned an application for parking requirement interpretations at a proposed Calverton indoor agriculture facility until October 22 pending an expert parking report.

Key actions

  • The board approved variances for Jean Monsees at 54 Cedar Street, Aquebogue, allowing reduced front, rear, and side yard setbacks and increased impervious surface coverage for a side and rear addition to her home.
  • The applicant testified the addition adds dining/rec room and closet space, does not add bedrooms, involves no wetlands or DEC jurisdiction, and neighbors did not object.
  • The board adjourned the application from 645 Grumman Blvd, LLC in Calverton until the October 22, 2026 meeting, pending submission of an additional consultant report on parking needs for the proposed indoor agricultural facility.

Discussed

  • The applicant for 645 Grumman Blvd, represented by an attorney and company principals, is proposing to convert existing industrial buildings on a 20-acre Natural Resource Protection zoned parcel into an indoor agricultural cultivation use, with no specific tenant or crop type yet identified.
  • The Central Pine Barrens Commission previously granted a hardship waiver limiting the site to a maximum of 211 parking spaces (196 existing/proposed plus 15 land-banked), reduced from an original approximately 305 spaces, with about 34,843 square feet of asphalt to be converted to green space.
  • The applicant proposed roughly 196 parking spaces based on an estimated 90 to 120 employees across the site, citing comparable indoor agriculture facilities elsewhere, but board members requested more detailed data, including consultant qualifications and calculation methods, given no code-specified parking standard exists for this use.
  • Board members and the applicant discussed that the facility could potentially be used for cannabis cultivation, and the applicant stated it is a landlord that will lease space to tenants who will handle interior build-out.
  • The applicant's attorney and representatives agreed to provide a follow-up report from their consultant, Third Act Architecture and Consulting, detailing qualifications, methodology, and comparable facility examples within about 30 days, ahead of the October 22 meeting.
