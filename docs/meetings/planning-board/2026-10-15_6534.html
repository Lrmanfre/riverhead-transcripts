Summary AI

The Planning Board advanced several wireless tower, subdivision, and bulkhead applications, approved a negative declaration and lead agency status for the Walmart Riverhead expansion and scheduled its public hearing for November 19, and deemed the Breezy Hill concrete-crushing facility's environmental review document incomplete.

Key actions

  • The board classified the Verizon 285 Hubbard Avenue wireless tower application as a Type 1 SEQRA action and will seek lead agency status.
  • The board assumed lead agency and issued a negative declaration for the Elite Towers Baiting Hollow 120-foot monopole tower proposal.
  • The board granted a Chapter 219 Coastal Erosion Hazard Area permit for the Sound Shore Guild Association to replace about 210 feet of bulkhead with new material.
  • The board assumed lead agency and issued a negative declaration for the Walmart Riverhead expansion and scheduled a public hearing on the project for November 19, 2026.
  • The board deemed the draft supplemental environmental impact statement for the proposed Breezy Hill Group asphalt and concrete crushing facility at 1792 Middle Road inadequate and set criteria for resubmission, including addressing the Zoning Board of Appeals' role as an involved agency.
  • The board granted 90-day extensions of minor subdivision approvals for the Mallas Property, Manor Lane, and Jets 139 LLC subdivisions.

Discussed

  • For the Elite Towers and Verizon tower applications, board members and applicants discussed whether to include a decorative American flag on the stealth monopole, with mixed opinions, and noted towers could later be raised an additional 10 feet under existing code without further board review.
  • For the Walmart expansion, the applicant revised plans to retain more parking lot landscaping, addressed transfer-of-development-rights requirements, updated building elevations per Architectural Review Board comments, and resolved reduced sewer and county traffic improvement requirements at the Route 58/Cromer Avenue intersection.
  • For the Andersson Acres major subdivision in Laurel, the applicant requested the board consider an alternative 33-lot sketch plan that would place all lots in one school district rather than split between two districts, but board members raised concerns about an overly long, narrow road layout; no decision was made and the matter will be revisited.
