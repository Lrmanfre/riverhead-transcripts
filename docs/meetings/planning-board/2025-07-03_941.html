Summary AI

The Riverhead Planning Board approved or advanced several commercial site plan applications on July 3, 2025, including a negative declaration for Venetia Square and preliminary approval for a cannabis dispensary that drew significant public opposition.

Key actions

  • The board issued a negative declaration under SEQRA and scheduled a public hearing for the Venetia Square commercial center proposal on Route 25A in Wading River, a long-running application for roughly 27,000 square feet of retail across multiple buildings.
  • A public hearing was scheduled for the BJ's Wholesale Club expansion at East End Commons, which includes a roughly 10,500-square-foot building addition and a 16-pump accessory fueling station.
  • Preliminary site plan approval was granted for a cannabis dispensary application (Resolution 2025-64); the vote passed with board members noting the site fully complies with town code and no variances were required.
  • The Tanger Outlet 1700 building conversion to a Shake Shack restaurant received preliminary site plan approval, with a condition that lighting in the vicinity be brought up to town code compliance.
  • Administrative site plan approval was granted for the Bargain Molding site plan amendment at an address on West Main Street, revising traffic circulation to two-way using only the existing eastern curb cut.
  • A public hearing was scheduled for the 940–946 West Main Street redevelopment, a revised plan for two new commercial warehouse buildings that no longer requires zoning variances.
  • A site plan application for a new 3,000-square-foot commercial retail building at 305 West Main Street was introduced to the board; the applicant will seek relief from the Zoning Board of Appeals for impervious surface coverage and a landscape buffer requirement before returning for a public hearing.

Public hearings & comment

  • Multiple residents spoke against the cannabis dispensary application, citing proximity to a hospital, senior communities, Timothy Hill Ranch, a church, and residential streets, as well as traffic concerns on Ostrander Avenue.
  • An attorney for the dispensary applicant noted the site meets all town and state requirements and that Riverhead's local cannabis regulations are more restrictive than state minimums.
