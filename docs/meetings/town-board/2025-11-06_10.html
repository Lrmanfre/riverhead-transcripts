November 6, 2025 — Town Board

Town Board Meeting

Summary AI

The Riverhead Town Board held a lengthy public hearing on the proposed 2026 budget, which would pierce the tax levy cap for the eighth time in nine years, and a separate hearing on Community Development Block Grant funding requests from local nonprofits. The board also moved through roughly two dozen resolutions, including formally appointing a new police chief.

Key actions

  • The board voted to appoint a new police chief to the Riverhead Police Department.
  • The board closed the public hearing on the 2026 preliminary budget and overriding the state tax levy cap, leaving written comment open until November 16.
  • The board closed the annual Community Development Block Grant public hearing, with written comment open until November 14; approximately $250,000 in federal HUD funds administered through Suffolk County is anticipated.
  • The board authorized the town clerk to seek bids for construction of a water district well and for general hardware items.
  • The board authorized a request for proposals for audit services for the Town of Riverhead Community Preservation Fund.
  • The board rejected bids for food and meat products and authorized a re-bid.
  • The board awarded a bid related to a landfill water main extension project.
  • The board authorized a professional services agreement for the Meeting House Creek stormwater, wetland, and habitat restoration project.
  • The board ratified a stipulation with a municipal employees union bargaining unit.
  • The board authorized the town clerk to publish notice for public hearings on proposed local law amendments to traffic regulations and to fire prevention enforcement.
  • A resignation of a public safety dispatcher and a maintenance mechanic were accepted, and amended employment terms for the town IT staff member were approved.

Public hearings & comment

  • A resident argued the proposed budget increase of approximately 6.74 percent above the cap is excessive and that the public hearing notice omitted required salary information; the finance administrator clarified that the cap is being pierced only in the general fund and that the majority of increases are contractual, including a roughly $3.1 million, or 10 percent, rise in the police department budget.
  • The finance administrator noted the town has a $23–24 million RAISE grant with a roughly $7 million local match obligation, which is a primary reason for preserving fund balance rather than using it to offset the operating budget.
  • Three nonprofits—a girls' triathlon empowerment program, Long Island Head Start, and the Riverhead Community Awareness Program—requested CDBG funding for youth services, playground shading at a local center, and a school-based social worker at Roanoke Avenue Elementary School respectively.
