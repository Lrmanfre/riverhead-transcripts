September 4, 2024 — Town Board

Town Board Meeting

Summary AI

The Town Board adopted the 2024 Comprehensive Plan update with agritourism resort language fully removed, approved dozens of routine resolutions, and announced plans for a public task force on farmland preservation.

Key actions

  • The board voted unanimously to adopt the 2024 Comprehensive Plan update, with all language referencing agritourism resorts on Sound Avenue removed prior to the vote.
  • A public hearing on a proposed advisory committee for the Community Preservation Fund water quality improvement program was held and kept open for written comment through September 14, 2024.
  • A previously scheduled public hearing on waterways regulations was cancelled and will be reposted with corrected information for the next meeting.
  • The board authorized execution of a grant contract with the state Office of Parks, Recreation, and Historic Preservation for up to $500,000 for an adaptive children's playground at the Riverfront.
  • The board authorized coordination with the Town of Brookhaven to pursue grant funding for dredging, bulkhead repair, and landscaping at the Wading River duck ponds.
  • The board authorized 2024 bow hunting for deer on town-owned properties at EPCAL, 1751 Sound Avenue, and Middle Road.
  • A resolution to formally establish the supervisor's EPCAL advisory committee by vote was withdrawn after insufficient board support; the supervisor stated the ad hoc committee will continue.
  • The September 18th public forum previously scheduled to discuss agritourism zoning was cancelled with no replacement date announced, though the supervisor indicated a community task force on farmland preservation will be formed.

Public hearings & comment

  • Numerous residents spoke in support of removing agritourism resort provisions from the Comprehensive Plan and several asked the board to delay the plan's adoption to allow further public review.
  • Speakers raised unresolved concerns about specific nonconforming-use provisions and proposed accessory dwelling unit size rules in the Comprehensive Plan.
  • A marine biologist urged the board to adopt stronger bluff setbacks along Long Island Sound to protect coastal resilience.
  • A housing counseling agency director thanked the board for expanding accessory dwelling unit guidelines, citing the change as a tool for homeownership affordability.

Discussed

  • Board members described the town's financial pressures, including roughly 6,300 acres of unprotected farmland still at risk, a 2015 bond not paid off until 2030, and a pending lawsuit over a large Southampton housing development whose students could enter the Riverhead school district.
  • A resident suggested revenue alternatives including a per-gallon surcharge on oil offloaded at local depots and impact fees on new development.
