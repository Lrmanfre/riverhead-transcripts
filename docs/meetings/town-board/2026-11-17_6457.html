Summary AI

The Riverhead Town Board held public hearings on parking code amendments, water district fine increases, and a handicapped parking spot, then adopted the 2027 annual budget and roughly 30 other resolutions including staff appointments, a disabled veterans tax exemption, and a withdrawn parking-enforcement RFP; public comment focused on a railroad station cleanup request and calls for the board to discuss immigration-related local law (ICE cooperation policy).

Key actions

  • The board adopted the Town of Riverhead 2027 annual budget.
  • The board adopted a local law granting a property tax exemption for 100% disabled veterans, affecting an estimated 200 Riverhead residents.
  • A resolution to seek a turnkey parking enforcement and permitting system was withdrawn until a vendor presentation at Thursday's work session.
  • The board approved several code amendment notices, including changes to landmarks/historic district penalties and a boards/commissions chapter.
  • Multiple staff appointments were approved, including highway department equipment operators, a bus driver, and a water treatment plant operator.
  • A new handicapped parking space was approved for the north side of 2nd Street near East End Arts, following a public hearing.

Public hearings & comment

  • A public hearing was held on amendments to five zoning code sections allowing shared-parking reductions in more districts, illustrated by a pending Riverhead Plaza restaurant redevelopment site plan.
  • A public hearing addressed steep fine increases (up to $10,000 for repeat offenses) for damaging water district property such as fire hydrants, prompted by recent tampering incidents causing discolored water.
  • Residents questioned how a parking district audit and new downtown parking management costs would be tracked separately from general town funds; officials said the district's finances are included in the town's overall audit.
  • Several residents, including Colin Tooker, Sarah Reynolds, and Kevin Shea, urged the board to publicly discuss a proposed local law on immigration enforcement cooperation (referred to as 'Ola law'), citing fear among Hispanic residents and lack of board response to prior inquiries.

Discussed

  • A resident asked about the deteriorating condition of the old Riverhead Railroad Station; officials said the Anti-Litter Committee is researching the site's history and will bring findings to a future work session.
  • Board members discussed a propane tank leak incident from the prior night that prompted a multi-agency emergency response, with further detail expected at Thursday's work session.
  • A resident raised concerns about maintenance of other town-owned historic buildings showing peeling paint and deterioration.
