July 21, 2026 — Town Board

Town Board Meeting

Summary AI

The Riverhead Town Board adopted roughly 30 resolutions covering parking rule changes, zoning amendments, RVAC's new ambulance facility lease, and several personnel and financial items. Public comment was dominated by debate over ICE enforcement practices and a local protective ordinance (OLA).

Key actions

  • The board adopted a local law prohibiting parking on the south side of Sound Avenue from Northville Turnpike east for 1,000 feet to address dangerous street-side parking.
  • The board closed a public hearing and immediately adopted a local law imposing two-hour parking limits (8 a.m.–11 p.m.) in the waterfront lots behind the restaurant currently known as Moonfish and along East Main Street toward McDermott Avenue.
  • The board adopted local law amendments to Chapter 301 (Zoning/Site Plan Review), removing certain fees from the code so the town board can set them by resolution, and streamlining the site plan process by eliminating a preliminary step.
  • The board approved a 75-year lease agreement (25 years plus five 10-year renewals) with Riverhead Volunteer Ambulance Corps for a new facility at 1111 Osborne Avenue, with RVAC covering bond and interest costs from ambulance fees; the project is described as not requiring direct taxpayer funding.
  • A public notice was scheduled for the next town board meeting to conduct a Monroe balancing test hearing for the RVAC ambulance barn, which slightly exceeds height and square footage limits.
  • The board authorized the police department to apply for DHS/FEMA funding to purchase a high-speed rapid-response patrol vessel.
  • A public hearing was authorized on amendments to the town's animal sales ordinance (Chapter 207) regarding commercially bred dogs, cats, and rabbits in pet stores.
  • Special event permits were approved for a canine competition at Veterans Memorial Park and for another event at the Acliro property.

Money

  • The receiver of taxes monthly utility report for June 2026 totaled $502,107.22; the building department monthly report for June 2026 was $107,462.
  • A $95,000 fund balance transfer was approved in the CDA meeting to cover additional legal fees related to the Triple Five litigation.
  • The town attorney noted that approximately $16 million of a previously awarded $24 million grant, plus $2 million from Suffolk County, is designated toward a downtown parking garage, with the balance intended to be funded from parking enforcement revenue.

Public hearings & comment

  • Both parking public hearings were left open for written comment until July 31; one hearing was subsequently closed by board vote so its local law could be adopted immediately.
  • Multiple residents and online speakers urged the board to adopt a local protective ordinance (OLA) addressing ICE operations; the board did not schedule a vote on OLA.
  • A resident raised questions about $207,000 paid to outside counsel for a lawsuit challenging the state's even-year election law; town attorney stated the town's withdrawal was strategic and that recovering the funds is unlikely given a signed contract.
