Summary AI

The Riverhead Town Board held five public hearings and approved roughly three dozen resolutions on June 3, 2025, with significant public debate over the proposed eminent domain acquisition of a leasehold at 129 East Main Street for a boutique hotel and the Vale Levitt venue selection process.

Public hearings & comment

  • A public hearing was held on designating 48 Willow Street in Aquebogue as open space and authorizing $75,000 from Community Preservation Funds, with a speaker clarifying the money was originally collected for wetland remediation and this resolution restores it to that purpose; written comment accepted through June 13.
  • A public hearing was held on amending the town zoning code's cannabis section to add the Business CR zoning district as a permitted location for dispensaries and on-site consumption businesses, and to codify the state's municipality-notification process; written comment accepted through June 13.
  • A public hearing was held on the Calverton Satellite Earth Station special permit and site plan at 317-391 Berman Boulevard, where a resident noted that 20 of the proposed 40 antennas were already installed without approval; town staff confirmed violations result in double the standard site plan fee; written comment accepted through June 13.
  • Public hearings were also held on amendments to zoning definitions for 'office' and 'personal services' and on a repeal and replacement of the town's public nuisance article, which expands enforcement procedures and increases fines; both open for written comment through June 13.

Key actions

  • The board approved a resolution adopting findings and determination under the Eminent Domain Procedure Law to acquire the leasehold interest in the ground-floor unit at 129 East Main Street, which is currently occupied by a bar, for the town square hotel development project.
  • The board approved a Water District capital project budget for an electrical upgrade at Plant No. 7.
  • The board authorized acceptance of Suffolk County Office for the Aging funds to implement the 2025 residential repair program for the elderly.
  • The board ratified acceptance of donated monument-relocation services from a local monument company that moved the Garfield Langhorne Congressional Medal of Honor statue to the front entrance of the new town hall.
  • The board approved a contract with a soccer training organization for the Police Athletic League fall 2025 soccer program.
  • The board awarded a rebid contract for sewer improvements at 203-213 East Main Street.
  • The board classified a special permit and site plan application for a motocross track at 2822 River Road, Calverton as a Type I action under SEQRA and requested lead agency status.
  • Multiple personnel actions were approved, including seasonal hires in recreation and traffic control, a GIS technician salary increase, a junior civil engineer provisional appointment, two custodial worker promotions, and a part-time bilingual clerk appointment.

Discussed

  • A resident requested the board issue a formal RFP for the sale and redevelopment of the Vale Levitt property; the supervisor stated the board voted three to two to proceed with a contract with the Jazz Loft and that vote will not be reopened.
  • Multiple residents and one online caller questioned the downtown hotel project, including its story count, inclusion of condominiums, IDA tax benefits for developers, and the adequacy of public engagement; board members defended the project as the product of six-plus years of public input and nearly $50 million in state and federal grants with no local taxpayer cost.
