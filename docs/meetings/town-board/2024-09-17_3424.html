Summary AI

The Riverhead Town Board held three public hearings — on a water main extension for a landfill solar project, a traffic code update, and rental dwelling unit regulations — then adopted 28 resolutions covering personnel, budgets, and public safety equipment.

Key actions

  • The board adopted all 28 resolutions on the agenda, including budget adjustments, personnel appointments, and public safety measures, with unanimous votes throughout.
  • A budget transfer using ARPA funds was approved to support police department vehicle purchases.
  • The Ambulance District received approval to transfer fund balance for radio replacements, enabling nearly all volunteer members to have radios at home to hear calls.
  • The Ambulance District also received approval to fund a chassis-and-body remount of an existing ambulance, estimated to cost significantly less than purchasing a new rig.
  • The board authorized an agreement with Language Line Services for the Riverhead Justice Court to provide interpreter access across multiple languages.
  • An officer was sworn in and promoted to the rank of sergeant in the Riverhead Police Department.
  • A local law amending the Community Preservation chapter of town code was adopted.
  • The board authorized a public hearing notice on a proposed amendment to the fire prevention chapter of town code.
  • The agritourism resort/spa provisions were confirmed to have been removed from the comprehensive plan, effectively ending that development proposal for Sound Avenue.

Public hearings & comment

  • A public hearing on a water main extension on Young's Avenue was opened and closed; a developer will pay approximately $450,000 to extend roughly 1,100 feet of 8-inch water pipe and install two fire hydrants at the town landfill solar and battery storage project, with construction expected to begin around January or February 2025.
  • A public hearing on a vehicle and traffic code amendment was held to codify an existing no-right-turn-on-red restriction at Cromer Avenue and County Road 58 that has been in place since approximately 2012–2013.
  • A public hearing on proposed amendments to Chapter 263, Rental Dwelling Units, drew substantial public comment; supporters cited fire safety and overcrowding concerns while opponents, including representatives from the NYCLU, a fair housing nonprofit, and a housing counseling agency, raised concerns about fair housing law, enforcement implications, and impacts on low-income and immigrant residents; all three hearings remain open for written comment until September 27 at 4:30 p.m.

Discussed

  • Board members emphasized that the rental code amendment targets landlords operating unsafe, illegally subdivided units rather than tenants or any particular group.
  • Riverhead Volunteer Fire Department representatives spoke in support of the rental regulations, citing dangers of basement and attic fires in illegally altered homes for both occupants and firefighters.
