Summary AI

The Town Board adopted 32 resolutions covering budget transfers, personnel actions, committee appointments, and new programs, while a public hearing on a Business PB zoning amendment for the Roanoke Avenue corridor drew public opposition and was kept open for written comment through August 28. Immigration enforcement activity and a proposed special-events code affecting agriculture dominated public comment.

Key actions

  • Three officers received proclamations for preventing a vehicle from striking pedestrians at the Polish Town Fair on August 16, 2026.
  • The board adopted a budget transfer increasing the 2026 legal-fees appropriation to $280,000, up from $150,000 (Resolution 765).
  • A Water District capital project budget for a Calverton property at 1001 Scott Avenue was adopted (Resolution 769).
  • The board authorized $380,000 in Community Development assistance for eligible first-time homebuyers (Resolution 774).
  • A Town of Riverhead Hazardous Materials Response Team was formally established and an emergency response plan adopted (Resolution 792).
  • The board approved live music at Wading River Beach for 2026 (Resolution 772).
  • Multiple Wildlife Management Advisory Committee members were appointed or reappointed, along with one Recreation Advisory Committee reappointment.
  • The board rescinded two prior resolutions (2026-605 and 2026-656) via Resolution 776, with no explanation stated in the transcript.
  • A license agreement was authorized allowing Riverhead Volunteer Ambulance Corps to use Town Hall basement space for EMT instruction and an annexed building for medical billing and storage (Resolutions 795–796).

Public hearings & comment

  • A public hearing was held on amending the Business PB Zoning Use District (Roanoke Avenue corridor) to add personal services as a specially permitted use with hours of 5 a.m. to 10 p.m.; the hearing was kept open for written comment until August 28.
  • Multiple residents spoke in opposition to the Business PB zoning amendment, including a representative of the Greater Calverton Civic Association.
  • A winery owner and the Long Island Farm Bureau executive director opposed a proposed special-events code, saying it is burdensome and unfairly targets agriculture; the Supervisor stated agriculture is being removed from the draft.
  • An Ag Advisory Committee letter read into the record stated the draft events code was written without meaningful community input, is highly restrictive, and increases fees and penalties on farmers.
  • A speaker urged the board to purchase a 30-plus-acre parcel adjacent to the Sound Avenue Preserve using Community Preservation Funds, noting the price per acre is less than what was paid for the preserve two decades ago and the CPF fund has sufficient money.
