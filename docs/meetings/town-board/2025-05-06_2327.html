May 6, 2025 — Town Board

Summary AI

The Riverhead Town Board held six fire code public hearings and voted unanimously on roughly 40 resolutions covering personnel, special events, parking, grants, and land use matters.

Key actions

  • The board adopted a budget amendment appropriating HUD HOME funds for a capital project.
  • A six-month extension of preliminary site plan approval was granted for a property at 38 McDermott Avenue.
  • The board authorized a SEQRA negative declaration and scheduled a public hearing for a proposed Calverton Satellite Earth Station at 317-391 Berman Boulevard, correcting a prior address error.
  • The board approved a license agreement with Waddington Realty Corp for use of town parking facilities, retroactively.
  • A license agreement was authorized allowing Stein Seafood LLC to operate floating upweller systems in East Creek.
  • Special event permits were approved for Duck Pond Day, Riverhead Country Fair, Dreamland Amusements East End Fair, Long Island Antique Power Association shows, and two other events.
  • The board authorized soliciting an RFP for the sale of 214 Griffin Avenue.
  • A public hearing was scheduled to consider designating a parcel at 48 Willow Street as open space using $75,000 in Community Preservation funds.
  • Robert Myrick was appointed to the town's Emerging Technology Committee.

Money

  • Tax collection totals reported as of May 1 stood at approximately $105.9 million.
  • The building department reported approximately $87,483 in permits for April.
  • A speaker publicly objected to the Waddington Realty parking license fee, calling it inadequate at roughly 31 cents per space per day.

Public hearings & comment

  • Six fire code public hearings were closed and kept open for written comment through May 16, covering new definitions, alarm reset restrictions, lithium-ion battery fire reimbursement, fire suppression requirements at gas stations, outdoor fire permits, and reclassifying certain violations.
  • A resident raised concerns about the pace of approval for the Dreamland Amusements fair, particularly regarding parking and the absence of a finalized license agreement; town officials said review by police, fire marshal, and other departments is ongoing.
