Summary AI

The Riverhead Town Board held promotions, community recognition, two public hearings, and voted on roughly three dozen resolutions at its October 1, 2024 meeting.

Key actions

  • The board promoted police Lieutenant Danielle Wilsey to captain, making her the first female captain in Riverhead Police Department history.
  • The board promoted a sergeant named Dino Isgro to the rank of police lieutenant.
  • The board classified the Elite Towers Wading River Fire District cell tower proposal at 1503 North Country Road as a Type 1 action under SEQRA and requested lead agency status.
  • The board authorized a public hearing to conduct the Monroe Balancing Test for the proposed Elite Towers cell tower at the Wading River Fire District, with the hearing scheduled for November 7.
  • A resolution authorizing an appraisal of town property by a named firm was tabled by unanimous vote.
  • The board approved a license agreement with the Butterfly Effect Project for use of the Riverhead Senior Center.
  • The board amended and approved preliminary site plan approval for an application identified as Hildreth Real Estate Advisors at 1001 Scott Avenue.
  • The board authorized the town clerk to post notice for a public hearing on a proposed local law amending the town code regarding removal and return of abandoned shopping carts.
  • The board amended the terms and conditions of employment for Police Chief Frost.
  • The board reclassified an assistant town engineer position to town engineer.

Public hearings & comment

  • A public hearing on the 2025 Community Development Block Grant funding application sought input on up to $250,000 in federal HUD funds for public services, home improvement, and public facilities; written comment accepted through October 14.
  • Representatives from Riverhead Community Awareness Program and the Butterfly Effect Project testified in support of CDBG funding for youth counseling and programming at local schools.
  • A public hearing was held on a proposed amendment to the Community Preservation Fund project plan to allow CPF funds to help finance water main extensions to areas with contaminated private wells; the water district superintendent said the immediate project on Forge Road would require roughly $193,000 in CPF money against approximately $1.2 million in grant funding.
  • Two speakers urged the board to tighten the amendment's language to explicitly cap water quality spending at the statutory 20 percent of annual CPF revenues, and raised concerns about preserving funds for farmland and a future transfer-of-development-rights bank; the deputy town attorney stated the 20 percent statutory cap in Town Law already applies and cannot be superseded.
