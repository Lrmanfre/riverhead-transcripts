Summary AI

The Riverhead Town Board held its June 18 meeting, passing roughly 30 resolutions covering personnel, contracts, events, and land-use matters, while residents raised concerns about a Calverton warehouse project, unpermitted construction at a local recreational business, and proposed agritourism zoning.

Key actions

  • The board approved a sewer district capital project budget adjustment.
  • The board rejected existing bids for residential solid waste collection and authorized re-bidding.
  • The board awarded a bid for a used tow truck.
  • A public hearing was scheduled for a special permit application at 4592 Middle Country Road, Calverton, to construct a two-family dwelling.
  • The board authorized publishing notice to extend the Calverton industrial moratorium, with a public hearing set for July 2 at 2:05 p.m.; the current moratorium remains in effect through approximately July 12.
  • The board assumed lead agency status and issued a negative declaration under environmental review for a site plan application at 1001 Scott Avenue, Calverton, and scheduled a public hearing on that application.
  • Preliminary site plan approval was granted for a commercial warehouse project at 901–923 Berman Boulevard, Calverton.
  • The board authorized a contract with New York State for a grant of up to $500,000 for a riverfront adaptive children's playground.
  • A resolution appointing a financial administrator was adopted.
  • Fireworks permits were approved for Riverhead Raceway on June 29 and August 10, 2024.
  • Resolution 567, related to forming a community preservation fund subcommittee following the Chapter 221 public hearing, was tabled pending recruitment of required community members.
  • Town Hall LED lighting conversion was approved.
  • Retirements of a fire marshal staff member and a traffic control specialist were accepted with board acknowledgment.

Public hearings & comment

  • A public hearing on a local law amending Chapter 221 to create a subcommittee focused on water quality and pollution prevention projects under the Peconic Bay Community Preservation Fund was held; no public comment was received.
