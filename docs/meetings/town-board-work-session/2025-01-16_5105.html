January 16, 2025 — Town Board Work Session

Town Board Work Session Meeting
CivicClerk page
Agenda PDF

Summary AI

The Town Board held a work session covering the December police report, formation of a new Hispanic community committee, a proposal to expand the Wesley Ackley Skate Park, and monthly reports from Justice Court, Code Enforcement, and the Town Clerk.

Key actions

  • The board expressed support for forming a new committee called the Hispanic Development Empowerment and Education Committee, with a resolution to formalize it planned; prospective members introduced include a community business leader, a local nursery owner, a former Guatemalan diplomat, a dentist with an education background, and a local pastor.
  • The board directed that a resolution be drafted authorizing the Community Development Agency to seek grants for a new cement skate park to be built on county land acquired through the Project Greenways program adjacent to the current Wesley Ackley Skate Park on Pulaski Street.
  • A presenter reported that a 501(c3) nonprofit has been established to support the skate park project and that grassroots fundraising has raised over $23,000 from more than 220 donors toward an estimated $1 million goal.
  • The Town Clerk noted the town is advertising to fill a part-time bilingual clerk position and suggested the new Hispanic committee may help identify candidates.

Money

  • Code Enforcement collected $41,525 in rental permit fees during December.
  • Code Enforcement conducted approximately 2,500 rental unit inspections in 2024 and issued approximately 715 summonses for the year.
  • The Town Clerk reported 2,723 FOIL requests processed through the clerk's office in 2024, not including police department requests, which the board noted represents a significant and growing cost to taxpayers.

Discussed

  • Captain Woolsey presented the December 2024 police report, noting vehicle thefts rose compared to December 2023, while robberies, burglaries, domestic incidents, and total summonses all decreased year-over-year.
  • The skate park proposal envisions a phased cement construction on land adjacent to Stotsky Park, with the existing skate park site potentially repurposed as a spray park or for other low-cost recreation; an ADA-accessible pump track was also discussed as a possible addition.
  • Justice Court was closed from December 16 through the first week of January; increased building permit and rental permit fines approved in December will begin appearing in January court data.
  • Code Enforcement opened 651 total complaints in 2024 and noted ongoing issues at a neglected commercial parking lot, including missing stop signs, deteriorated pavement, and graffiti, with cases pending in Justice Court.
  • The Town Clerk reported processing over 350 marriage licenses, more than 500 notarizations, 63 special permits, and over 50 new or amended local laws in 2024, and said new agenda-management and FOIL software is expected to be operational by June.
