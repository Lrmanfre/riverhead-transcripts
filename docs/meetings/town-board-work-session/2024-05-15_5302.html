May 15, 2024 — Town Board Work Session

Town Board Work Session Meeting

Summary AI

The Riverhead Town Board work session covered code changes, department reports, a new boat rack program, sewer upgrades, and a full slate of routine appointments and resolutions. A special public hearing on the comprehensive plan is set for May 20.

Key actions

  • A code amendment to Chapter 301 was advanced for publication that would count pervious parking (gravel, permeable pavement) as impervious coverage for zoning purposes, closing a workaround that allowed applicants to avoid impervious-coverage variances.
  • The board moved to schedule a public hearing and bond authorization amendment for a biosolids reuse project at the sewer district, projected to save roughly $860,000 per year against an estimated loan payment of about $845,000 per year at zero interest through the Environmental Facilities Corporation.
  • A new seasonal boat rack program at South Jamesport Beach was approved at $150 per season for 12 spaces, open to town residents and taxpayers on a first-come, first-served basis.
  • The board directed staff to move forward with a $75,000 surveillance camera installation at East Creek Marina, funded from the East Creek fund.
  • A resolution was listed to resurface the basketball court at Stotsky Park, with board members also requesting landscaping cleanup around the court beforehand.
  • The police department reported receiving a state grant of approximately $1.47 million for technology, expected to cover the body-camera project over five years.
  • A code enforcement proposal to use a third-party service to identify unlicensed short-term rentals (Airbnb, VRBO) was discussed as a potential revenue tool.
  • The board was notified of a Water Infrastructure Improvement Act grant application due June 15 for the biosolids project.

Money

  • Code enforcement collected approximately $44,850 in rental permit fees in April, with year-to-date revenue of about $90,400.
  • Justice Court reported fire marshal, quality-of-life, and neighborhood-preservation convictions totaling several thousand dollars in April, with illegal-dumping trials carrying minimum fines of $1,500 now being set down.
  • Resolutions included multiple budget transfers for the municipal garage tow truck purchase, water district capital projects, and Veterans Memorial Park sanitary system.

Public hearings & comment

  • A special town board meeting with two public hearings on the comprehensive plan update and draft DGEIS is scheduled for Monday, May 20 at 6 p.m., in person and via Zoom, with written comments also accepted.

Affects residents

  • Community Day at Stotsky Park is Sunday, May 19, 10 a.m. to 2 p.m., free and family-friendly, with no rain date.
  • Bathroom facilities at Veterans Memorial Park are close to becoming operational after health department permits were secured for the sanitary system installation.
