August 15, 2024 — Town Board Work Session

Town Board Work Session Meeting

Summary AI

The board canceled the August 20 agritourism public hearing due to high public opposition and scheduled a public forum for September 18 at 6 p.m. to allow open dialogue. The board also reviewed proposed new rental property regulations targeting overcrowding, illegal bedrooms, and parking conditions.

Key actions

  • The public hearing on proposed agritourism zoning changes, originally set for August 20, was canceled and replaced with a public forum on Wednesday, September 18 at 6 p.m. in the town board room.
  • A special board meeting was announced for Wednesday, August 21 at 6 p.m. to consider adopting the comprehensive plan update and issuing a findings statement.
  • A proposed amendment to Chapter 263 of the town code would prohibit habitable bedrooms in basements of two-family and multi-family rentals, ban third-story attic bedrooms, require driveway documentation with rental permit applications, prohibit parking on lawns, limit commercial vehicles to two per property, and require a rental permit before advertising a unit for rent.
  • The board reviewed a simple code change to add Cromer Avenue at County Road 58 to the list of locations where right turns are prohibited, at the request of Suffolk County.
  • The board reviewed 28 proposed resolutions covering personnel appointments, equipment purchases, a local law on bed-and-breakfast facilities, front-yard parking restrictions, and other administrative matters.
  • Blues by the River, previously scheduled for September, was noted as canceled.

Money

  • Code enforcement collected approximately $36,425 in rental permit fees in July, bringing the January–July total to approximately $188,825.
  • Justice Court mandatory surcharges for blight mitigation totaled approximately $1,125 out of roughly $12,000 in total fines and surcharges reported for the period reviewed.

Discussed

  • Board members discussed increasing rental property violation fines, noting the current first-offense maximum of $500 to $1,500 may not be a sufficient deterrent for landlords renting overcrowded units.
  • Code enforcement reported 55 new complaint investigations, 85 summonses issued, and inspections of approximately 131 residential structures and 75 apartment units in July.
  • The town clerk reported 244 FOIL requests received in July and said a meeting with Brookhaven staff was scheduled to address issues with the board's minute-management software.
