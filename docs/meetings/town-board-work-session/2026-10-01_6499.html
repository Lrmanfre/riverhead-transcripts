October 1, 2026 — Town Board Work Session

CivicClerk page
Agenda PDF

Summary AI

The board heard updates on the 4-H property preservation effort and discussed a proposed payment-in-lieu-of-parking (PILOP) program for downtown development, then moved to a large resolutions agenda and executive session on legal and contractual matters.

Discussed

  • The Peconic Land Trust and Open Space Committee gave a timeline update on preserving the 4H property, saying Suffolk County's purchase of development rights likely won't close until early 2027 rather than the originally hoped-for December date.
  • Board members questioned why a roughly 32-acre portion of the property being purchased with Community Preservation Fund money was appraised separately at a higher per-acre value than the whole parcel, and were told it reflects the land trust combining smaller purchases before reselling the subdivided piece to the town.
  • The town's downtown revitalization consultant presented a proposal for a payment-in-lieu-of-parking (PILOP) program, recommending developers pay $18,000 per parking space (up from a 2020 recommendation of $12,500) for projects without existing site plan approval, with funds placed in a dedicated account for future parking infrastructure.
  • The consultant presented figures showing existing downtown residential projects already have an estimated 144-278 space parking deficit, with hundreds more units in the pipeline (including Heatherwood and a planned hotel) likely to add to that gap.
  • Board members asked for more detail on future parking needs, including from the planned development at 111 East Main Street, before deciding on the PILOP proposal, and raised questions about whether PILOP funds could help fund a downtown shuttle.
  • Officials discussed delays in the downtown parking garage project, noting a federal RAISE grant contract (needed to unlock about $15 million in funding) is now expected in the first quarter of 2027, and discussed designing the garage to be built in expandable phases to control costs.
  • A councilwoman asked about adding striped interim parking spaces downtown during construction, including a proposal along Heidi Behr Way requiring relocation of a fire hydrant.

Key actions

  • The board was presented with a large pre-final resolutions packet covering budget adjustments, donations, personnel appointments, bid extensions, contracts, and local law amendments, to be voted on at a future meeting.
  • Notable items included a resolution to publish an RFP for redevelopment of a destination attraction at 111 East Main Street, a public hearing on a water main extension on Scott Avenue in Calverton, and grant funding through Assemblywoman Giglio for a brush fire staging area at Veterans Memorial Park.
  • The board voted to enter executive session to discuss the Hock v. Town of Riverhead legal matter and a contractual agreement between the Town and New York State.
