October 22, 2026 — Town Board Work Session

CivicClerk page

Summary AI

The Riverhead Town Board reviewed monthly department reports, discussed a proposed zoning change to expand shared-parking reductions for shopping centers, and reviewed a draft update to landmark preservation fines before heading into executive session.

Key actions

  • The board agreed to move forward with a public hearing on amending town code to allow a 20% shared-parking reduction in more commercial zoning districts, including large shopping centers like those with Costco, Home Depot, Target, and BJ's.
  • The board plans to schedule a public hearing on updated Landmark Preservation Commission fines for unauthorized alteration or demolition of historic landmarks, with the demolition fine range proposed at $10,000 to $15,000 (with possible increase to $20,000) and alteration fines proposed between $1,000 and $5,000 for a first offense.
  • The town clerk had a new secure public notice board installed outside town hall by the Buildings and Grounds department.

Discussed

  • Police Chief Frost reported crime statistics were similar to 2025, with criminal incidents dropping about 19% from August to September, though petit larceny (shoplifting) increased.
  • Code enforcement reported 62 complaints opened in September, including violations for illegal apartments, unpermitted construction, litter, and zoning use, with 36 violations issued.
  • The town justice reported September court fines totaled $51,350, with blight mitigation and fire marshal violations as the largest categories.
  • Board members raised concerns about how a 20% parking reduction would be verified and reassessed if a new, higher-traffic business later moved into a shopping center, and about how outdoor retail space (e.g., garden centers, rental equipment displays) at large stores is taxed relative to building footprint.
  • Board members discussed strengthening demolition penalties for landmark structures, citing the demolition of the former Spicy's building as an example of an inadequate existing fine.

Affects residents

  • Residents were warned about a private company sending mailers soliciting water testing, which officials said is not affiliated with the health department or EPA and is being used to sell unnecessary filtration systems; officials said town water remains safe and monitored by the DEC.
  • Upcoming community events include a fall cleanup and plogging event on November 7, a pumpkin smash on Young's Avenue, a rescheduled trunk-or-treat on October 23 at Stotzky Park, a repair cafe and kids' movie night on October 24, and a drive-in movie on October 30.
