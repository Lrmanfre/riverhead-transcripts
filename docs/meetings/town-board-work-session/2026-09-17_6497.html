The Riverhead Town Board work session on September 17, 2026 covered department reports (police, justice court, code enforcement, town clerk) and a presentation on a proposed public sculpture honoring first responders, before moving into executive session on real property and personnel matters.

Discussed

  • The police chief presented July and August incident reports showing increases in criminal incidents, simple assaults, shoplifting, and DWI arrests compared to the prior year, attributed largely to summer activity; alarm billing revenue has resumed after being manually processed.
  • Board members and the chief discussed the impact of state bail reform laws, noting officers spend hours processing arrests only for many individuals to be released, and discussed limits on notifying federal immigration authorities under current state law.
  • The town justice court reported combined July-August collections of about $63,750 from town code and parking fines, with an emphasis on compliance and one trial held each month; body cameras have helped with open alcohol and public urination cases.
  • A board member raised concerns about long-vacant blighted buildings on Main Street and requested code revisions to increase fines beyond the current $5,000 commercial penalty, which was referred for further review.
  • Code enforcement reported 179 complaints investigated in July-August, with property maintenance violations the largest category, two new part-time code officers hired, and increased proactive patrols and transient rental enforcement using new evidence rules.
  • Rental program statistics were shared, including 128 applications and about $77,290 in revenue collected in July-August, with year-to-date totals of 538 applications and about $294,605 in revenue.
  • The town clerk reported increases in hunting, fishing, and marriage licenses, and said the town is exploring AI transcription services to eventually produce verbatim meeting minutes.
  • An artist presented a proposed sculpture, 'Grateful Turnout,' made from decommissioned fire hose and turnout gear to honor first responders, proposed for a location on Osborn Avenue near McDermott Avenue, funded by a New York State grant with no cost to the town.

Key actions

  • The board indicated support for moving forward with the first responder sculpture donation, pending final location approval and coordination on installation with town staff.
  • The board went into executive session to discuss two real property disposition matters and a personnel matter involving a change in an employee's status.
