January 11, 2024 — Town Board Work Session

Town Board Work Session Meeting

Summary AI

The Town Board work session covered the comprehensive plan's environmental review scoping process, a complex state-mandated change to senior/disability property tax exemption income definitions, proposed agritourism zoning code amendments aimed at preserving Sound Avenue farmland, updated recreation facility rental fees, and police department year-end reports showing lower crime but ongoing dispatcher staffing shortages.

Key actions

  • The board is expected to adopt a final scope for the comprehensive plan's Generic Environmental Impact Statement at the upcoming Tuesday meeting, advancing the SEQRA process.
  • The board will need to pass a local law with a public hearing before March 1 to define how senior citizen and disability property tax exemption income is calculated under a new state real property tax law change.
  • A public hearing on proposed agritourism zoning amendments (Chapter 301) is being prepared; the code would allow inns and resorts on a minimum 100 acres with 70 percent of the land preserved for active agricultural production.
  • Recreation facility rental fees will increase from $30 to $35 per hour for 2024, with new charges of $20 per field for ball field prep and $25 per week for multi-purpose field prep.
  • Free 2024 beach stickers for veterans and volunteer fire and ambulance members are being ratified by resolution.
  • A public hearing on amendments to the town's parking and towing regulations for privately owned, publicly accessed lots is set for February 6 at 2:05 p.m.
  • Reeves Beach has been closed following storm erosion that made the gazebo, walkway, and boat ramp unsafe; town engineering is assessing repairs and storm damages are being documented for potential federal assistance.

Money

  • The assessor warned that raising the senior exemption income ceiling to the state maximum of $58,400 could reduce the town's assessed tax base by approximately $4 million, shifting the tax burden to other property owners.
  • Recreation facility rentals generated $73,000 in 2024 revenue with a goal of reaching $100,000 by end of 2024; proceeds are reinvested in fields and programs.
  • A $750,000 letter of credit performance bond from the Villas at Roanoke Seacrest Estates development was accepted by resolution.
  • A $3,000 stipend for a network and systems specialist related to the town's VoIP telephone system is being ratified.

Discussed

  • The assessor explained that the new state income definition for property tax exemptions is highly complex, varies by taxing jurisdiction, and cannot reliably predict individual outcomes; the board must set a definition and income limit before holding a required public hearing.
  • Agritourism amendments were revised to prohibit temporary structures and catering events such as weddings, limit agricultural events to six per year, require events to be related to on-site or regional Long Island farming, increase Sound Avenue setbacks from 100 to 250 feet, and cap resorts at 100 guest units per 100 acres.
  • Police year-end reports showed overall criminal incidents were lower in 2023 than 2022 despite rising calls for service; dispatcher staffing remains critically short at 10 of a needed 16 positions, and the board discussed whether pay increases could reduce costly overtime.
