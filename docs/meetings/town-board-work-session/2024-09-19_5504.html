September 19, 2024 — Town Board Work Session

Town Board Work Session Meeting
CivicClerk page
Agenda PDF

Summary AI

The Riverhead Town Board held a work session covering anti-litter recognition, police and code enforcement monthly reports, Justice Court and Town Clerk updates, and a summer recap video before moving to executive session.

Key actions

  • The Anti-Litter Committee presented Certificates of Appreciation to Riverhead Building Supply, the Business Improvement District, the Riverhead Rotary Club, and the East End Hiking Club, with two additional recipients absent.
  • The board discussed restoring a provision in the sign ordinance that would allow code enforcement to impound illegally posted signs without permits, and directed staff to pursue a code revision.
  • The board indicated it may review adding a fee for repeat rental-property inspections when violations are not corrected, and noted the current no-show fee of $50 may be too low.
  • The Town Clerk reported plans to move forward with Civic Plus agenda-management software, with an expected three-to-four month implementation timeline, and noted a possible 20% increase in clerk's office revenue through service adjustments.

Discussed

  • The Police Chief explained that a transition to a new NIBRS-compliant records management system in May changed how crimes are categorized and counted, making year-over-year comparisons unreliable; July recorded 228 criminal incidents and August recorded 235.
  • Narcan was used once in July and once in August by officers, with additional civilian doses administered in August; one fatality occurred in July at a hotel where the subject was already deceased before officers arrived.
  • Code enforcement opened 77 new complaint investigations in August, issued 85 summonses, inspected 101 residential structures and 160 apartment units, and collected approximately $38,875 in rental fees in August for a year-to-date total of roughly $227,700.
  • Justice Court collected approximately $29,593 in August, including mandatory surcharges, and is scheduling trials for older rental-permit cases where property owners have not complied.
