July 16, 2026 — Town Board Work Session

CivicClerk page
Agenda PDF

Summary AI

The Riverhead Town Board held a work session covering police and Justice Court monthly reports, a county down payment assistance program for first-time homebuyers, and a slate of upcoming resolutions, before moving into executive session.

Key actions

  • The board reviewed 27 draft resolutions to be voted on at the next Town Board meeting, covering topics including water and sewer district budget adjustments, police vessel grant application, local law amendments to zoning and fee procedures, special event approvals, and a seasonal pump-out boat operator appointment.
  • A public meeting of the helicopter task force was announced for 4 p.m. the same day, moved to the main board room to accommodate more residents.
  • The board discussed opening a concurrent town down payment assistance program alongside Suffolk County's program, with approximately $280,500 available to assist up to 10 first-time homebuyers purchasing homes in Riverhead, with individual awards of $28,050 each and an eligible purchase price up to $646,000.
  • A public hearing was noticed for a Monroe balancing test related to the Riverhead Volunteer Ambulance Corps ambulance barn at 1111 Osborne Avenue, with the board indicating a preference to close the hearing promptly to keep the project on schedule.
  • A 99-year lease agreement between the town and the Riverhead Volunteer Ambulance Corps is being finalized and is expected to appear in next week's packet.
  • The board entered executive session to discuss personnel matters, a license agreement with a childcare provider, a contractual agreement with Riverhead Central School District, a real property purchase, and litigation involving American Breeders.

Discussed

  • Police reported 3,249 total incidents in May and 3,136 in June, with domestic incident numbers notably higher in May compared to the prior year; the chief said no specific cause was identified.
  • Justice Court collections in May were approximately $56,000, roughly double the prior May figure of $29,000, attributed to fee and fine increases and more active prosecution; a default judgment of close to $30,000 was obtained in June after a defendant failed to appear for a jury trial.
  • The town clerk noted plans to reorganize agenda formatting so correspondence letters appear at the bottom rather than inline, to make resolutions easier to locate online.
