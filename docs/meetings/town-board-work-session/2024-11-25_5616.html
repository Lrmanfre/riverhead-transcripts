November 25, 2024 — Town Board Work Session

Town Board Work Session Meeting

Summary AI

The Riverhead Town Board work session covered monthly reports from police, code enforcement, town clerk, and justice court, discussed showmobile rental fee increases, heard a public notice about a Calverton development project requiring an environmental impact study, and reviewed 34 resolutions ahead of a Tuesday vote.

Key actions

  • The board reviewed 34 draft resolutions including approvals for special events, water district capital projects, bid authorizations, and a 2025 meeting schedule.
  • A resolution would authorize shotgun deer hunting on town property at Enterprise Park at Calverton from January 5 to January 31, 2025, with the walking and bike trails closed during that period.
  • A resolution would assume lead agency status and issue a negative declaration for an Elite Towers cell installation at the Wading River Fire District on North Country Road, finding it exempt from local zoning requirements under the Monroe balancing test.
  • The board discussed significantly raising showmobile rental fees from the current $100 per day, noting a six-hour event costs the town approximately $1,300, and directed staff to develop a tiered fee schedule for 2025 that could distinguish between nonprofits and town-sponsored events.
  • The town clerk reported a part-time bilingual Spanish-speaking clerk will begin Monday and work daily from 11 a.m. to 2:30 p.m.
  • All downtown holiday events—the Lions Parade, tree lighting, bonfire, and Santa—have been consolidated onto a single date, December 7, 2024.

Discussed

  • Planning staff announced a public scoping hearing for TJOC Calverton, a proposed mixed-use development on a roughly 16-acre site on Middle Country Road in Calverton that would include seven residential lots, about 30,000 square feet of ground-floor commercial space, and 36 one-bedroom apartments; the hearing is December 5 at 6 p.m.
  • The CCBT committee discussed an upcycling concept for construction debris at EPCAL and a food scrap diversion program that staff estimated could save up to $15,000 per month in tipping fees.

Money

  • Code enforcement reported collecting $35,850 in rental permit fees in October and $311,325 year-to-date.
  • Justice court reported $40,980 in total collections for October.
