February 15, 2024 — Town Board Work Session

Town Board Work Session Meeting

Summary AI

The Town Board held a work session featuring monthly reports from the Police Department, Justice Court, and Code Enforcement, and previewed a slate of resolutions for the February 21 town board meeting. The previously scheduled Agritourism public hearing was unanimously canceled and will be rescheduled.

Key actions

  • The board unanimously agreed to cancel and reschedule the Agritourism public hearing that had been set for February 21.
  • A resolution to rescind an earlier water district extension public hearing and replace it with a corrected one was discussed, due to tax map number errors in the original resolution.
  • The board discussed going out to bid for a cloud-based VoIP phone system to replace the current on-premises system, with preliminary indications of cost savings.
  • A resolution to support the Suffolk County District Attorney's campaign to change state laws on fentanyl possession thresholds was previewed for a vote on February 21.
  • A resolution authorizing up to $8.5 million in matching funds for a federal RAISE 2024 transportation grant application was included in the packet.
  • Purchase of a 2024 Kenworth dump truck for the Highway Department at a cost of $278,827.96 was on the resolution list.
  • Rental permit fees collected in January totaled $20,575, and Justice Court fines for the month totaled $16,000.
  • Monthly reports from Police, Justice Court, and Code Enforcement will now be presented at every third-Thursday work session.

Discussed

  • Code Enforcement reported investigating approximately 79 complaints and issuing roughly 38 tickets since January 1, with over 1,500 rental properties requiring annual inspection.
  • A county-wide records management system called Niche is expected to go live around April or May, allowing real-time data sharing among Suffolk County law enforcement agencies.
  • An online rental permit portal called OpenGov is targeted to launch March 1, allowing residents to apply, pay fees, and schedule inspections online.
  • The board discussed adding a Code Enforcement officer on evening shifts to allow faster response when police identify violations during overnight tours.
  • Police overtime costs have declined over the past two years and are approaching pre-COVID levels, attributed in part to increased staffing.

Affects residents

  • Residents can file code complaints anonymously online.
