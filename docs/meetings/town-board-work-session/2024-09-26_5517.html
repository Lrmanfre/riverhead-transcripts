September 26, 2024 — Town Board Work Session

Town Board Work Session Meeting

Summary AI

The board reviewed the 2025 tentative budget, which pierces the state tax cap by 4.14% for the general fund, and discussed a proposed 190-foot cell/communications tower at the Wading River Fire Department headquarters that would improve emergency radio and cellular coverage.

Key actions

  • The supervisor presented the 2025 tentative budget; total appropriations for the town operating fund are $109,519,600, an increase of about $5.9 million over 2024.
  • The general fund exceeds the state tax cap by 4.14%; all other taxing districts remain within the cap.
  • The board discussed initiating SEQRA Type 1 classification and scheduling a Monroe balancing-test public hearing for Elite Towers' proposed 190-foot communications pole at the Wading River Fire Department headquarters on North Country Road.
  • Staff indicated two resolutions would be prepared for the October 1 meeting: one to classify the cell tower project as a Type 1 SEQRA action and one to schedule the Monroe hearing, tentatively set for October 16.
  • The police department accepted a donation of two used Harley-Davidson motorcycles from Suffolk County.
  • A police department employee, Danielle Wilsey, was promoted to captain.
  • The board approved a final CSEA 2023–2025 contract and a new dental plan MOA covering multiple bargaining units.
  • Resolutions advanced a road-opening permit fee increase from $200 to $300 and an abandoned-cart fee increase from $125 to $175.

Money

  • For a property valued at approximately $720,000, the proposed town-wide tax increase amounts to roughly $288 per year.
  • Residential solid waste costs would rise approximately $5.25 per month.
  • Major drivers of the general fund increase include police salaries, New York State retirement rate hikes, a 9% health insurance increase, new vehicles, debt service on the town hall building, and new radio equipment required by Suffolk County's bandwidth change.

Discussed

  • Fire commissioners and the Elite Towers attorney described the Wading River area's poor radio and cell coverage as a direct public-safety problem, citing inability to transmit cardiac telemetry and loss of radio contact during active structure fires.
  • Board members discussed tracking how approved development projects affect the town's tax base over time as a way to offset future tax increases.
