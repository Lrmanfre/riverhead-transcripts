May 1, 2025 — Town Board Work Session

Town Board Work Session Meeting
CivicClerk page
Agenda PDF

Summary AI

The Town Board reviewed proposed updates to the public nuisance code, previewed a new online bill-pay portal for the Water District, and discussed a state parks grant application for the town square playground. A long list of personnel and administrative resolutions was also previewed.

Key actions

  • The board directed staff to prepare a resolution scheduling a public hearing on proposed revisions to the public nuisance code (Chapters 251 and 252) that would broaden the definition of public nuisance beyond the current requirement of criminal convictions.
  • Board members agreed to raise the minimum Justice Court fine for public nuisance violations from $500 to $1,000, with further discussion on the full penalty schedule.
  • Staff presented a new online bill-pay portal for Riverhead Water District customers that will allow account lookup, one-time payments by e-check or credit card, and autopay enrollment with no fee; May bills are planned to go out with notice of the new service.
  • The board previewed a resolution ratifying an application for a New York State Municipal Parks and Recreation Grant of up to $1 million for improvements to the town square playground, with a 10% match from Community Development Block Grant funds; the application is due the following day.
  • A resolution authorizing pursuit of Suffolk County Downtown Revitalization Round 23 funding for improvements to the Third Street municipal parking lots was included in the resolution packet.
  • The board previewed a resolution to publish a request for proposals for the sale of 214 Griffin Avenue, a town-owned property adjacent to town hall.
  • The board went into executive session to discuss matters related to acquisition of real property.
  • An update on board committee activities was postponed to the following week's work session.

Discussed

  • Staff explained that the revised public nuisance code adds defined terms, structured inspection and due process procedures, civil penalties of $5,000 to $10,000 per day in Supreme Court actions, and explicit First Amendment and 911-call protections that were absent from the prior code.
  • Board members and staff discussed the challenge of funding park maintenance through grants, which generally require new capital improvements rather than repairs; a board member noted draft legislation has been brought to a state senator to allow a portion of park and recreation fees to be used for maintenance.
  • Staff discussed potential projects for a second round of the state parks grant later in 2025, including lighting at a town ball field and improvements at Stotsky Park, noting that concrete cost estimates and a match source are required before applying.
